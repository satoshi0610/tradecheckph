Two different regulators cover two different questions, and mixing them up is where a lot of confusion starts. The SEC's advisories are about who is allowed to solicit investments (see our [SEC advisory list guide](/guides/sec-advisory-list-explained/)). The BSP's rules are about something else entirely: how money is allowed to move across the border.

## What the BSP actually regulates

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) governs foreign exchange transactions conducted through Philippine banks and BSP-authorized agents. It does not license or regulate forex/CFD brokers themselves — that separation matters, because it means BSP compliance and SEC-advisory status are two independent things to check, not one.

## Is it legal to send money to an offshore broker?

Sending funds abroad to fund a trading account with a legitimately operating offshore broker is not itself illegal, provided:

- The funds come from a legitimate, documentable source (savings, salary, other lawful income).
- The transfer goes through normal, authorized banking channels — a licensed bank or BSP-authorized remittance/payment channel — rather than an informal or unauthorized transfer arrangement.
- The transaction complies with standard anti-money laundering (AML) requirements, which for practical purposes means banks may ask you to document the purpose and source of larger transfers.

## Why this is a separate check from broker legitimacy

Following BSP-compliant transfer procedures says nothing about whether the broker receiving your funds is legitimate or properly regulated where it operates. You need both: a compliant way of sending the money, and a verified, regulated broker on the receiving end (see our [broker license verification guide](/guides/how-to-verify-a-forex-broker-license/)). Treating "I used my bank's normal transfer process" as proof the broker itself is safe is a common but mistaken assumption.

## Practical points worth understanding

- Large or unusually structured transfers may prompt your bank to ask for documentation — this is a normal part of AML compliance, not automatically a sign of a problem on your end.
- Using informal, unauthorized transfer arrangements to move trading funds (rather than your bank's standard international transfer or an authorized remittance channel) removes the regulatory protections that come with using proper channels, and can itself create compliance issues independent of the broker's legitimacy.
- BSP rules and thresholds are updated periodically — treat this article as a general framework and confirm current specifics either with your bank directly or on the BSP's official site before moving large amounts.

## The practical takeaway

- BSP rules govern how you move money across the border; they don't certify that a receiving broker is legitimate or regulated.
- Use your bank's standard international transfer process or another BSP-authorized channel, with a documentable, legitimate source of funds.
- Check broker regulatory status separately, using the steps in our [license verification guide](/guides/how-to-verify-a-forex-broker-license/).

*This is general educational information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Foreign exchange regulations are updated periodically — always confirm current rules directly with your bank or on the official BSP website (bsp.gov.ph) before transferring significant funds.*
