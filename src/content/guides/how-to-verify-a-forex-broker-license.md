The single most reliable way to avoid a forex scam isn't a "top 10 broker" list — it's learning to check a license yourself, directly on the regulator's own website, in under five minutes. Here's how.

## Step 1: Find the license number

Legitimate regulated brokers display their license or registration number somewhere on their site, usually in the footer or on an "About" / "Regulation" page. If you can't find one at all, that alone is a red flag.

## Step 2: Go to the regulator's official register — not a link the broker gives you

Type the regulator's domain in yourself:

- **FCA (UK):** the Financial Services Register on fca.org.uk
- **ASIC (Australia):** ASIC Connect's professional registers on asic.gov.au
- **CySEC (Cyprus):** the Regulated Entities register on cysec.gov.cy

Search the license number or company name directly on these official registers. Scammers sometimes list a real license number that belongs to a *different* company, so confirm the registered company name matches exactly what the broker uses to open your account.

## Step 3: Check the entity, not just the brand

Many international brokers operate several legal entities under one brand name — one regulated by a top-tier regulator, another registered in a jurisdiction with lighter oversight. Read carefully which entity you'll actually be contracting with based on your country of residence; it's often disclosed in the account-opening terms, not the marketing homepage.

## Step 4: Cross-check against the regulator's warning list

Most regulators, and the Philippine SEC separately, also publish a list of flagged or unauthorized entities. If a broker or a look-alike name appears there, stop. See [what an SEC advisory actually means](/guides/sec-advisory-list-explained/) and a worked example in [Is Exness Legal in the Philippines?](/guides/is-exness-legal-in-the-philippines/)

## Step 5: Treat "regulated" claims on comparison sites with caution — verify yourself

Even well-intentioned comparison content can be outdated. Before funding an account, the register lookup in Step 2 is the only step that matters.

## Red flags that go beyond licensing

- Guaranteed or "risk-free" returns
- Pressure to deposit quickly or increase your deposit to "unlock" a bonus
- Requests to trade through an unfamiliar third-party platform instead of the broker's own regulated platform
- Difficulty finding any physical company registration details

Once you've confirmed the licence, one question remains: which legal entity actually holds your account, and does any compensation scheme cover it? That's covered in [what happens if your broker goes bust](/guides/what-happens-if-your-broker-goes-bust/).

Use the steps above on any broker you're considering — including brand names you already trust.
