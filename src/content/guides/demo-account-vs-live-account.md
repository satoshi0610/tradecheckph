Opening a demo account before funding a live one is good practice — but it's easy to walk away from a demo with false confidence about things it was never designed to test.

## What a demo account is good for

- **Platform familiarity** — learning the order types, charting tools, and mobile/desktop interface without financial risk.
- **Strategy testing under simulated conditions** — checking whether your trading plan is mechanically sound before risking real capital.
- **Spotting obvious execution issues** — a platform that's confusing, glitchy, or missing basic features in demo mode is a real signal.

## What a demo account can't tell you

- **Real execution quality** — demo environments often execute orders in idealized conditions. Slippage, requotes, and execution speed during real volatility can differ meaningfully from what you experience in a simulator.
- **Real withdrawal experience** — the single most-reported broker complaint (see our [common forex scams guide](/guides/common-forex-scams-in-the-philippines/)) is withdrawal friction, which by definition a demo account cannot test at all.
- **Psychological pressure of real money** — trading decisions under simulated money and trading decisions when your own capital is on the line are documented to differ; a strategy that performs well on demo doesn't guarantee the same discipline live.
- **Customer support responsiveness** — how a broker treats a demo user and how it treats a funding, live client are not necessarily the same experience.

## A safer path from demo to live

1. **Test the platform and your strategy on demo first** — this is still worth doing, just with realistic expectations about what it proves.
2. **Verify the broker's license directly on the regulator's register** before funding anything live (see our [license verification guide](/guides/how-to-verify-a-forex-broker-license/)) — a demo account tells you nothing about regulatory legitimacy.
3. **Start live with the smallest amount the broker allows**, specifically to test real execution and, eventually, a real withdrawal — before committing meaningful capital.
4. **Test a withdrawal early**, even a small one, rather than waiting until you have a large balance. This is the step most traders skip, and it's the step that actually reveals whether a broker's stated withdrawal process works as described.

## The practical takeaway

- A demo account is a legitimate first step, but treat it as a platform and strategy test — not a broker legitimacy test.
- Regulatory verification and an early small live withdrawal test are what actually protect you; a smooth demo experience doesn't substitute for either.
